The AI Landscape and Choosing the Right AI
1. Question and Scope
- Understand the six types of AI: descriptive, diagnostic, predictive, prescriptive, generative, and agentic AI.
- Explain the purpose, working approach, strengths, and limitations of each type.
- Explore real-world applications used by organisations and identify how they benefit businesses.
- Develop a decision guide to select an appropriate AI type based on a business problem.
- Apply the guide to three problems faced by ShopSmart, an online shopping store, and recommend which AI it should implement first.

2. Findings: The Six Types of AI
Descriptive AI
Definition: Descriptive AI analyses past and current data and presents the results in reports, charts, or dashboards. It helps a business understand what has happened.
Real-world examples:
1. Wipro Enterprises uses sales data and dashboards to help managers understand business performance.
2. Central Group uses customer insights to understand customer feedback.
Strength: It makes business data easier to understand and helps managers identify trends and performance changes.
Limitation: It mainly explains what has happened. By itself, it may not explain the cause of a problem or predict what will happen next.

Diagnostic AI
Definition: Diagnostic AI examines data to investigate why something happened. It helps identify possible reasons behind changes, failures, or unexpected results.
Real-world examples:
1. Central Group analyses customer feedback to understand customer concerns.
2. Wipro Enterprises uses sales information to identify areas that need improvement.
Strength: It helps businesses investigate problems and make decisions based on evidence instead of guesses.
Limitation: Finding a relationship or pattern does not always prove the actual cause. Additional investigation may be needed.

Predictive AI
Definition: Predictive AI uses historical data and machine-learning models to estimate what may happen in the future.
Real-world examples:
1. Tchibo uses AI to forecast product demand and manage stock.
2. Papa John's uses data and AI technologies to help predict customer orders.
Strength: It helps businesses prepare for future demand, manage stock, and plan resources in advance.
Limitation: Predictions can be inaccurate when historical data is incomplete or market conditions and customer behaviour change.

Prescriptive AI
Definition: Prescriptive AI recommends an action by considering available information, business goals, and possible choices. It helps answer the question, “What should we do?”
Real-world examples:
1. Wipro Enterprises uses AI to recommend which products stores should stock. 
2. UPS uses its ORION system to find efficient delivery routes.
Strength: It can help businesses select more efficient actions, reduce waste, and use resources effectively.
Limitation: The recommended action may not be suitable if the data, assumptions, or business objectives are incorrect. Human review may still be required.

Generative AI
Definition: Generative AI creates new content, such as product descriptions, text, images, summaries, or answers, based on patterns learned from data.
Real-world examples:
1. Amazon uses generative AI to help sellers create product descriptions.
2. Central Group uses generative AI to help create product descriptions.
Strength: It can save time when creating content and provide faster assistance to customers.
Limitation: It may produce incorrect details or misleading answers, so generated content should be checked before being used.

Agentic AI
Definition: Agentic AI can work towards a goal by planning steps, using tools, and taking actions with some level of independence. Unlike a system that only provides an answer, an agent may also perform tasks.
Real-world examples:
1. Amazon's shopping assistant helps customers find products and supports certain shopping actions.
2. Kohl's uses an AI gifting assistant to help customers find suitable gifts.
Strength: It can handle several connected tasks and reduce repetitive work for customers or employees.
Limitation: An agent may misunderstand a request or take an unsuitable action. It needs appropriate permissions, safeguards, and human oversight.

3. A decision guide
Checklist before choosing AI:
- Clearly define the business problem and the expected result.
- Check whether the required data is available, accurate, and sufficient.
- Compare the cost, complexity, and expected benefits of possible solutions.
- Consider privacy, security, accuracy, and the need for human supervision.
- Start with a small test and measure the results before expanding the solution.

4. Application to ShopSmart
Problem 1: Understanding sales performance
Suitable AI: Descriptive AI
ShopSmart may have many orders and products but may not clearly understand which products sell the most or which months generate higher sales. A descriptive AI dashboard could summarise revenue, order numbers, best-selling products, and monthly sales trends.

Problem 2: Predicting product demand
Suitable AI: Predictive AI
ShopSmart may experience stock shortages for popular products and excess stock for products with low demand. A predictive model could estimate future demand using previous sales, seasonal trends, promotions, and product availability.

Problem 3: Answering customer questions
Suitable AI: Generative AI
ShopSmart may receive many repeated questions about product features, delivery times, returns. A generative AI assistant could provide answers using the store's verified product information and policies.

5.	Recommendation
I recommend that ShopSmart build a descriptive AI sales dashboard first. It will help the store understand its sales, popular products, and business performance. It is a practical starting point because it can use existing sales data. Later, ShopSmart can add predictive AI for stock management and generative AI for customer support.

6. Limitations
- AI results depend on the quality of the available data.
- Predictions and generated answers may be incorrect.
- AI systems can involve costs, privacy risks, and technical challenges.
- AI systems may need regular updates and maintenance.
- AI may give biased results if the data is unfair or incomplete.
